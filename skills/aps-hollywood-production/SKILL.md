# APS Hollywood Production

## Purpose

Translate approved stories into production-ready scenes while maintaining character truth, continuity, realistic causality, and usable rights documentation.

## Workflow

1. **Identify the production and canon.** Read the latest approved series bible, character records, timeline, episode outline, scripts, and asset register. Prefer current locked decisions over older drafts. If an essential fact is missing, mark it as an open question or a labeled proposal; do not invent locked canon.
2. **Separate fiction from real APS.** Treat fictional cities, businesses, leaders, institutions, events, and expansion plans as story-world material. Never present a fictional plot point as a real APS initiative.
3. **Build the scene.** Define scene purpose, point of view, location/time, entrances and exits, conflict, turn, consequence, and link to the next beat. Keep dialogue natural and causality believable. Flag contrivances, continuity gaps, and behavior that feels out of character.
4. **Prepare production assets.** Break approved scenes into shot-sized units with duration, framing, camera movement, blocking, lighting, sound, performance, and continuity notes. Maintain character appearance and location references across shots. Separate generated media from verified footage.
5. **Check constraints and rights.** Confirm age-appropriate treatment, performer/likeness consent, music and footage rights, commercial-use license, and provenance for every external or generated asset intended for release. A tool subscription alone is not proof that a particular asset has commercial rights.
6. **Review the cut plan.** Check screen direction and geography, audio continuity, pacing, edit transitions, and that the sequence communicates the intended story without unsupported exposition.
7. **Deliver reviewable outputs.** Provide the script or breakdown, asset/rights notes, assumptions, unresolved canon questions, and the next production step.

## Current series guardrails

Apply these when the work is for the established Elm City / Kingdom of Shadows slate; they do not automatically apply to unrelated projects:

- The current standard episode target is 12 minutes. The premiere crossover has its own longer event format; use the current approved schedule for exact timing.
- Preserve approved character motivations, relationships, timelines, institutions, and plot mechanics. For example, Micah's gift is prophecy and spiritual insight, not healing.
- Keep criminal and political plot causality grounded. Use implication where approved canon calls for it; avoid casual deaths among family, school, work, and community characters.
- For secret affairs, preserve the established “no face, no case” visual rule.
- Keep community and hospital stories realistic, emotionally specific, and distinct from spectacle.

## Media-generation handoff

For each shot, include:
- scene and shot ID
- intended duration and aspect ratio
- character/location continuity references
- action and camera direction
- dialogue, room tone, and sound design
- negative constraints and prohibited visual changes
- generation tool and model only if known and authorized
- commercial rights/provenance status

Mark mock footage, reference stills, and unlicensed preview material clearly. Never claim that a rendered scene is finished when only a prompt, storyboard, or still exists.
